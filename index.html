Case summary

Investigating the psychological crime scene

The Damage Report skips the DSM and starts with the evidence you actually live with. We frame internal struggles through real-life reactions instead of clinical labels, so you can laugh at the survival tactic and finally see where it came from.

The single unbreakable rule of the production

"The coping mechanism is funny. The suffering isn't."

We do not punch down at the trauma. We punch horizontally at the ridiculous survival tactics our brains invented to deal with it.

*Painted on the studio wall.*
Primary targetThreat assessments on footsteps. Strategic restaurant seating.
Tread carefullyThe relationship disasters. Collateral damage.
Off limitsThe trauma itself. No jokes.
Joke about it: the absurd things we do to survive.
Handle with care: what it did to the people around us.
Never joke: the abuse, the PTSD, the actual suffering.

House rules

Dark humor only works when people trust you with the dark part. These are the rules every episode has to pass before it leaves the basement.

Final operational compliance checklist

  • [ ]Are we diagnosing listeners or family members?No.
  • [ ]Are we treating every personality trait as trauma?No.
  • [ ]Are we laughing at the suffering?No.
  • [x]Are we laughing at our ridiculous survival tactics?Yes.
  • [x]Are we letting uncomfortable silence breathe?Yes.
  • [x]Does the episode examine the human experience instead of a DSM definition?Yes.

We speak from our own experience

We aren't qualified to diagnose anyone's family with a personality disorder based on our childhoods, so we don't.

Never on the show"He was definitely a narcissist and that's why he did everything."
What we say instead"I experienced him as unpredictable, and I became very alert to changes in his mood."

Silence is a tool, not an editing mistake

When something heavy lands, we leave three seconds of dead air before anyone speaks. If every painful moment gets covered with a joke, you learn that vulnerability isn't allowed here.

We call in the adults

TikTok psychology is not established science. When we hit the edge of what we know, a real clinician joins us to answer the questions, not to lecture.
